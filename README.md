# Course Repeat - Student Performance Correlational Analysis

**Project Overview**
---

This project examined the relationship between course repetition and student academic performance to determine whether repeat enrollment patterns were associated with lower cumulative GPA outcomes. I analyzed historical student records and conducted a regression analysis to identify the strength and direction of the relationship between course repeats and academic performance while exploring factors that may contribute to student success.

**Question:**
---


Is there a correlation between course repeats and student academic performance?

**Project Objectives**
---


- Review historical student course data
- Identify meaningful correlations between course repeats and student performance
- Support strategic decision making
- Communicate findings to senior leadership

**Data Sources**
---

This project utilized institutional data collected through internal reporting systems

- Student demographic information
- Enrollment records
- Academic performance indicators

**Methodology**
---

- Linear regression test listing GPA as the dependent variable and course repeats as the covariates. 
- Under “Model Fit,” I selected F test to yield results for comparative fit. 
- The independent variable is course repeats and is continuous. For this study, the independent variable has two levels: level one - one course repeat; and level two – two or more course repeats. 
- The dependent variable is GPA and is also continuous.
- Independent sample t-test to measure if there is a significant difference between the means of students with one course repeat and students with two or more course repeats

**Tools Used**
---

- Excel
- Tableau
- Jamovi

**Key Findings**
---

- Moderate positive relationship between course repeats and DFW grades. Each additional course repeat was associated with an increase of approximately 0.61 DFW grades for host institution repeats and 0.60 for external repeats.
- Each additional course repeat was associated with a 0.03 decrease in student GPA, although course repeats accounted for only approximately 1% of the variation in GPA.
- Stronger relationship between external course repeats and delayed academic progress. Each additional external course repeat was associated with approximately 2.52 additional credits behind, compared with 0.60 credits for host institution course repeats.
- External course repeats were associated with an increase in major term gaps, while host institution course repeats were associated with fewer major term gaps.
- Slightly higher average course repeats among male students and slightly lower averages among Pell-eligible students.

**Business Recommendations**
---

- Monitor course repeat patterns to identify students who may be at greater risk of academic difficulty and connect them with support resources earlier in their academic journey.
- Increase access to tutoring, supplemental instruction, and academic coaching for students enrolled in repeat courses.
- Use course repeats as one component of a broader student success framework that also considers enrollment status, transfer status, program of study, academic history, and demographic factors.
- Regularly review course repeat trends to evaluate the effectiveness of intervention programs and identify emerging student success challenges.

**Analytical Limitations**
---

While the analysis identified a meaningful relationship between course repeats and GPA, the regression model indicated that course repetition alone does not fully explain variation in student academic performance.

The model's R² value (coefficient of determination) suggested that a substantial portion of GPA variation was attributable to factors not included in the analysis. Potential influences may include:

 - Full-time versus part-time enrollment status
 - Academic major
 - Transfer student status and transfer shock
 - Credit load
 - Student demographics
 - Prior academic preparation

As a result, the findings should be interpreted as evidence of correlation rather than proof of causation

**Conclusion**
---

The analysis found a meaningful relationship between course repetition and academic performance, suggesting that students with higher numbers of repeated courses tend to experience lower academic outcomes. While course repeats may serve as an effective early indicator of academic risk, academic performance is influenced by a range of additional factors, highlighting the importance of a holistic approach to student success assessment and intervention
